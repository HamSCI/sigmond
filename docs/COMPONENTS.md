## The groups

**Core clients** are the DASI2 station's primary instruments: the `dasi2`
profile installs and runs them by default. **Additional clients** extend the
same station to other HF science, installed at the operator's discretion,
either together during bring-up (`smd bringup dasi2 --with-optional`) or one
at a time (`smd install <name>`, which installs and enables it; see
[Adding a discretionary client later](COMMANDS.md#adding-a-discretionary-client-later)).
**Station infrastructure** keeps the radio reachable and the station
reachable. **Libraries** serve the clients; the shared Python ones
(ka9q-python, hamsci-dsp, callhash) are on PyPI. **Native builds** are
compiled sources sigmond pins and builds on the station.

## Notes the table cannot hold

- **hf-timestd** also serves as the station's timing authority: it publishes
  the RTP-to-UTC offset and tier the other RF clients label against.
- **wspr-recorder** and **psk-recorder** upload to wsprdaemon.org, which
  forwards copies to wsprnet.org and pskreporter.info; a station posts to
  neither directly.
- **hfdl-recorder** sends ground-station squitter timing to wsprdaemon.org;
  its airframes.io feed stays off unless the operator turns it on.
- **mag-recorder** reads its magnetometer over USB-I²C, the one client that
  does not receive through radiod. Every RF client receives through
  ka9q-python.
- **ka9q-web** comes from a fork until its audio change merges upstream.
- **chirp-recorder** will take over from codar-sounder; it has not yet
  entered a release.

Every client runs in its own Python environment, manages its own systemd
services, and writes decoded spots and products to the shared SQLite sink
(`/var/lib/hs-uploader/sink.db`, or `/var/lib/sigmond/sink.db` on older
stations), from which `hs-uploader` ships them.

> **Why a station is worth more as part of a network:** each DASI2 node already
> carries several GPS-co-registered ionospheric instruments — `hf-timestd`
> (dTEC), `hf-tec` (coded-beacon absolute TEC), `codar-sounder` (oblique
> sounding / MUF), `hfdl-recorder` (dense opportunistic paths), and
> `mag-recorder` (the geomagnetic *driver*) — so a single node is already a
> multi-modal observatory. Replicated across the map, the mesh becomes a
> distributed-aperture instrument that can *image* travelling ionospheric
> disturbances, sense TEC gradients by differencing receivers that share a
> transmitter, and feed tomographic TEC — and it is resilient to the steady loss
> of the HF time standards it listens to (CHU went silent in 2026). See
> [STATION-NETWORK-CAPABILITIES.md](STATION-NETWORK-CAPABILITIES.md).
